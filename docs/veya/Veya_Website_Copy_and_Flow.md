# Veya Website Copy and Flow

**Version:** 1.0  
**Date:** September 7, 2026  
**Purpose:** Source document for website strategy, page flow, interface copy, checkout, FAQ, and agent implementation

## Website objective

The site should make a parent think:

> My child will actually enjoy using this—and for $1, I want our family to help shape it.

The primary conversion is a **$1 one-time Founding Family early-access pass** purchased by a parent or guardian.

The site should communicate, in this order:

1. What Veya does.
2. Why children want it.
3. Why parents can trust it.
4. Why the girls created it.
5. How it works with programs families already use.
6. Exactly what the $1 buys.

## Positioning

### Primary positioning

Veya is the beautiful home for a child's real-world progress: badges, adventures, skills, service, and goals.

### Supporting positioning

Created by two girls who wanted a better way to see everything they were learning—and what they could explore next.

### Relationship to Girl Scouts

Girl Scout badge tracking should be a visible and proud use case, not Veya's entire identity.

Use:

> Created by two Girl Scout members and designed to help families organize progress toward the badges and achievements they already love.

Do not use before authorization:

- Official Girl Scouts logos or badge artwork.
- “Official Girl Scout app.”
- “Approved by Girl Scouts.”
- “In partnership with Girl Scouts.”
- A headline presentation that could make Veya appear owned by GSUSA.

## Voice

Veya should sound:

- Hopeful, capable, and warm.
- Intelligent enough for parents without sounding corporate.
- Inspiring to girls without talking down to them.
- Proud of the founders.
- Curious and worldly.
- Clear at checkout.

Avoid:

- Legalistic language in prominent marketing sections.
- Fear-based safety messaging.
- Overexplaining intellectual property.
- Calling children “users.”
- Making Veya sound like homework management.
- Promising official awards, certification, partnerships, or launch dates that are not confirmed.

## Recommended navigation

- Why Veya
- How It Works
- For Families

On mobile, keep the early-access button visible after the visitor has passed the hero.

### 8. Privacy and trust

**Headline:**

> Their progress belongs to your family.

**Body:**

> Veya is being built around parent-controlled accounts, private-by-default progress, and thoughtful use of data. We do not need to turn childhood into an advertising profile to make a wonderful product.

- Children's progress is private by default.
- No behavioral advertising to children.
- Straightforward access and deletion controls.
- Card information handled by the payment provider, not stored by Veya.

Do not claim “we collect no data” unless that is technically verified across hosting, analytics, payments, authentication, email, logs, and embedded services.

## Checkout flow

### Step 1: Offer review

Display before collecting payment:

> **Founding Family Early Access**  
> **$1 one-time payment**
>
> Includes a priority early-access invitation, founder updates, and opportunities to help shape Veya. This is not a subscription.

Include the expected access window or active-development statement.

### Step 2: Adult information

Collect only what is needed:

- Parent or guardian email.
- Parent or guardian name if required by the payment provider.
- Country or billing location if required for payment or tax.
- Payment details collected directly by the payment provider.

Do not collect the child's name, birthday, troop number, school, location, badge progress, or photograph during purchase.

### Step 3: Required acknowledgements

Use unchecked checkboxes:

> I am a parent or guardian, or I am at least 18 years old.

> I understand that I am purchasing a $1 one-time early-access pass and that Veya is still in development.

> I agree to the Early Access Terms and Privacy Policy.

Optional marketing must be separate:

> Send me occasional Veya stories, family learning ideas, and product news.

Do not require optional marketing consent to purchase.

### Step 4: Payment button

**Button:**

> Pay $1 and Join Early Access

Do not label it merely “Continue” or “Join,” because the button should make the charge unmistakable.

### Step 5: Confirmation

**Headline:**

> You're a Veya Founding Family.

**Body:**

> Thank you for believing that children should be able to see—and help shape—their own growth. We sent your receipt and early-access details to **[ADULT EMAIL]**.

**Next actions:**

- Read the founders' latest update.
- Tell us what your family currently tracks.
- Return to Veya.

If asking what the family tracks, make it an optional adult survey. Do not ask a child to submit personal information from the confirmation page.

## FAQ copy

### What is Veya?

Veya is a family learning and progress platform for badges, skills, projects, service, and real-world adventures. It helps children understand what they are working toward, record what they have done, and decide what to explore next.

### Who created Veya?

Veya was conceived by two Girl Scout members who wanted a better way to track their own badge progress. They are building it with their mom and a small group of families, designers, developers, and creative tools.

### Is Veya an official Girl Scouts app?

No. Veya is an independent progress companion created by Girl Scout members. It can help families organize their work, but official Girl Scout materials and leaders remain the authority for badge requirements and awards.

### Can Veya tell us whether a Girl Scout badge has been earned?

Veya can show what a family has recorded and help organize progress. It does not officially certify or award Girl Scout badges. Families should use their official materials and normal troop or council process.

### Does Veya replace the badge booklet or official materials?

No. Veya provides a clear planning and progress view designed to be used alongside the relevant official materials.

### Is Veya only for Girl Scouts?

Yes — for now. Veya is built for Girl Scout families. Later we hope to welcome more people and kinds of real-world learning, while keeping Girl Scouts beautifully supported.

### What does the $1 include?

The one-time $1 Founding Family pass reserves priority early access, provides founder updates to the adult purchaser, and creates opportunities for your family to test Veya and share feedback.

### Is the $1 a subscription?

No. It is a one-time early-access payment. Any future optional paid plan will be described separately and will require a new, clear choice.

### When will we receive access?

Use one of these answers:

**If an estimate exists:**

> Early-access invitations are expected to begin [MONTH / SEASON / YEAR]. We will send progress and timing updates to the adult purchaser.

**If no estimate exists:**

> Veya is in active development. We will send progress updates and invitation timing to the adult purchaser as soon as they are available.

### Is the $1 refundable?

If Veya cannot offer your family early access, the $1 will be refunded. Before launch, a parent may request a refund by contacting [SUPPORT EMAIL].

### Does my child purchase early access?

No. A parent, guardian, or adult must complete checkout and manage the family account.

### How does Veya protect children?

Veya is being designed around parent-controlled accounts, private-by-default progress, limited data collection, and no behavioral advertising to children. The Privacy Policy will explain exactly what information is used and why.

### Will Veya always cost $1?

The $1 is for the Founding Family early-access offer. Veya may offer free or paid plans later, but joining early access does not enroll a family in a subscription.

## Implementation notes for website agents

1. The primary CTA must always include `$1` on the button or immediately beside it.
2. Never hide “one-time,” “not a subscription,” or the development status below the fold.
3. Do not collect children's information on the public site or checkout.
4. Do not display official Girl Scouts logos, badge images, handbook imagery, or uniform insignia without written permission.
5. One clear Girl Scouts independence disclosure is enough on the main page; repeat it in FAQ and footer, not in every section.
6. Keep organization-specific language in CMS/configuration fields so it can be updated without rebuilding the site.
7. Do not fabricate testimonials, family counts, partnerships, waitlist numbers, founder quotations, launch dates, or product capabilities.
8. Separate transactional access emails from optional marketing consent.
9. Provide working Privacy, Terms, Refunds, Contact, and Parent Support links before charging anyone.
10. The website should celebrate the daughters as founders while protecting their identifying details.

## Pre-publish checklist

- Replace `[MONTH / SEASON / YEAR]` or use the active-development alternative.
- Replace `[SUPPORT EMAIL]` with a monitored adult-managed address.
- Confirm exactly what the purchaser receives immediately.
- Confirm the refund process can actually be completed through the payment provider.
- Verify the payment button says `Pay $1`.
- Verify recurring billing is disabled.
- Verify receipt and early-access confirmation emails.
- Verify tax and payment-provider settings for the selling entity.
- Verify no child information is requested.
- Verify the Privacy Policy matches the actual analytics, email, hosting, payment, and authentication setup.
- Verify all displayed product features are live or clearly marked as upcoming.
- Verify all achievement artwork is Veya-original or licensed.
- Verify organization-specific copy can be remotely edited.
- Test the full flow on mobile.
- Ask both founders to read the page and answer: “Does this feel like the thing we are building?”
